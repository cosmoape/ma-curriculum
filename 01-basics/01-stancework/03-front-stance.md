# Front Stance

## Overview
The front stance drives your rear hand attacks and the inside slip. In Chinese, it's called 弓步, or "bow stance", apparently because the legs make a shape like a bow. It's basically a lunge. 

## Instruction
1. From horse stance, pivot and push off of the ball of your rear foot, as if squashing a bug or cigarette. Your rear foot turns so that it now points directly forward, with its heel pointing backwards.
2. Allow this rear foot pivot to turn your rear leg, hips, and shoulders about 90 degrees, so that they are now facing directly forward.
3. If needed, you can pivot your front foot slightly on its heel so that you end up in a comfortable lunge-like position.
3. Your weight should remain mostly split, with more weight on your front leg, roughly 70-30, with your head placed roughly over your front heel. 

## Checklist
- [ ] You should feel well-balanced, able to bounce, and come back into horse stance.
- [ ] Work on making this turn snappy and powerful. You should be able to switch back and forth between horse stance and front stance cleanly, without wobbling.
