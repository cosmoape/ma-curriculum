# Shifting Through Stances

## Overview
Now that you've learned and gotten a feel for the four stances - neutral, horse, front, and reverse - it's time to put them all together! 

None of these stances are static positions. In fact, ideally, we should be constantly moving through each of the four stances as we move around, throw punches, and defend. 

## Exercises
1. Start by practicing the basic stance transitions:
- Back and forth between neutral stance and horse stance.
- Back and forth between horse stance and front stance.
- Back and forth between front stance and reverse stance.

2. When you're comfortable with the basic transitions, practice cycling through all of the stances:
- Neutral to horse to front to reverse, and back to neutral again.

3. Reverse the cycle:
- Neutral to reverse to front to horse, and back to neutral again.

4. Now freestyle! Try switching from any stance to any other stance, and practice moving freely. Work on being smooth and fluid.

## Application
Notice that as we move through these stances, we're also moving our head! Horse stance lowers our head, front stance moves our head forward and slightly to one side, reverse stance moves our head backward and slightly to the other side. In this way, our stances allow us to move our head in all directions - up, down, forward, backward, left, and right. These movements basically turn into defense head movement. 

Also, the movement of our hips turning back and forth, will turn into the drivers for our punches (and kicks).

Finally, the alternating weight shifting of front stance and reverse stance can be incorporated into footwork. We can "walk" between front and reverse stances. 

Essentially, these four basic stances and moving through them becomes the basis for all of our fighting movements - attacking, defending, and moving. Better yet, we can actually *combine* offense, defense, and movement into every single movement!
