# Neutral Stance

## Purpose
The neutral stance is your default stance and your home base. Here we will learn the most fundamental (and maybe most neutral) neutral stance. Eventually every fighter will develop their own preferred variation, whether that is more bladed/square, more forward/backward, etc, but this is where we start.

## Instruction
1. Start with your feet about shoulder-width apart, facing forward.
2. Take a step back with your right foot if orthodox, with your left foot if southpaw.
3. Rotate both feet 45 degrees (to right if orthodox) to turn slightly side-on.
   - [ ] The toes of your front foot should align roughly with the heel of your back foot.
4. Split your weight evenly, 50-50 between your feet.
5. Bend your knees slightly so that your weight shifts onto the balls of your feet, and off your heels. 
   - [ ] You should be able to bounce lightly up and down with your feet and knees, and even squat and squat jump comfortably.
6. Raise your fists to about eye level, while keeping your elbows pointed at the ground.
7. Curve your back slightly and tuck your chin.

## Checklist
- [ ] Weight is split 50-50
- [ ] Toes of front foot align with heel of back foot
- [ ] Feels comfortable - can bounce, squat, and jump

## Application
Notice that the neutral stance is "in the middle" in a lot of aspects. Your feet are neither completely front and back, nor left and right. Your knees are neither completely bent nor completely straight. By being in the middle of every axis, you can move in any direction equally quickly, whether that is forward, backwards, left, right, up, or down. That's what makes this home base.
